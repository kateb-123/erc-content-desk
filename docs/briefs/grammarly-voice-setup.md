# Grammarly custom voice: ERC research briefs

Rewritten Oct 22, 2026, from VOICE.md and Kate's rulings that day. Paste the pieces into Grammarly's "Create your own voice" form.

---

## Do not use a preset

The Scholar preset (Formal, Precise, Objective) lifts plain words into report words ("rose" to "increased", "set aside" to "allocated"). Kate's ruling is plain words, so use the custom voice below.

---

## Custom voice: fields to paste

### 1. Voice name
```
ERC Research Brief Voice
```

### 2. Description
```
A neutral report for a policymaker's staff, not an argument. Plain past-tense
sentences that say what the study found, each claim backed by a number and its
context where the paper gives one (for example, "an increase of roughly 40% over a
baseline rate of 21.7%"). Plain everyday words where they are exact: rose, set
aside, before it began. Units in full ("percentage points", "standard
deviations"), the % sign, statistics and the sample size in digits, small counts
of things in words. Causal verbs only for a lottery or cutoff design, and never
firmer than the paper's own wording; the paper's hedges stay. Interpretations are
credited to "the authors"; never "we". No acronyms the reader has to carry, no
praise words, no idioms, no em dashes. The body stays even; the highlights and the
closing line may carry a little energy, never past what the study found.
```

### 3. Writing samples

Both are from published ERC briefs, as printed.

**Sample 1: Bowen, Making Room for the Arts (October 2026), Key Findings 1 and 2**
```
Expanded arts learning reduced disciplinary infractions. Students with
disabilities in initiative schools were 3.0 percentage points less likely to
receive a disciplinary infraction during the school year (Table 1). Previously,
9.7% to 13.9% of these students had received an infraction. The reduction was most
notable among middle school students, who were 10.2 percentage points less likely
to receive an infraction, and students with learning disabilities, who were 7.0
percentage points less likely. Elementary students showed no significant change.
The reduction was statistically significant for male students, but not for female
students. The authors suggest that arts learning may help students manage complex
emotions, supporting theories that it fosters self-control.

Attendance improved, especially for students with autism. Students with
disabilities in initiative schools had a daily attendance rate 0.5 percentage
points higher than those in comparison schools, equal to about one additional day
per school year. The largest gain was for students with autism, whose attendance
increased by 2.7 percentage points. An earlier study of the same initiative found
no attendance effect for the general student population, so the attendance benefit
showed up only for students with disabilities. The authors view improved
attendance as a strong indicator of engagement and belonging, areas where students
with disabilities often face challenges.
```

**Sample 2: Meer, The Economics of Encouragement (June 2026), Research Overview** (two sentences left out, marked [...], that overstate the paper)
```
This brief draws on records for 10,600 students who took Principles of
Microeconomics at Texas A&M University, a large, asynchronous online course that
serves as a gateway for many majors. The data span the fall 2017 through spring
2023 semesters and combine course rosters, registrar records on majors and minors,
and projected earnings by major. Of these students, 1,802 received an
encouragement email.

After final grades were posted each semester, one professor emailed roughly the
top 10% of students. The email praised the student's performance, told them they
were among the top performers in the class, and encouraged them to take more
economics courses and to consider majoring or minoring in the field. It also
linked to academic advisors. Importantly, students did not know there was a cutoff
or where they ranked in the class. They knew only their own letter grade.

This feature makes the study credible: because students could not see the cutoff,
researchers could compare students just above and just below the email threshold,
two groups otherwise very similar except one received the email and one did not.
This regression discontinuity design isolates the email's effect from other
student characteristics. [...] However, the study has limitations. The large,
online course format may not generalize to smaller or in-person classes. While the
dataset covers over 10,000 students, relatively few majored or minored in
economics, so those effects are less certain.
```

## Running the Humanizer on a draft

1. Open the brief's prose in Grammarly (brief-for-grammarly.md from the desk, or paste the text).
2. Choose **ERC Research Brief Voice**.
3. Run the Humanizer, then read every change. Check that:
   - numbers and percentages are unchanged;
   - causal claims still match the design and are no firmer than the paper;
   - the paper's hedges survived;
   - plain words were not lifted (rose, set aside, about);
   - no "we" and no em dashes were added.
