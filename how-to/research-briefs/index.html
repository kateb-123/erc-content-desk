How to

Make a research brief

From the paper to a live brief, in the desk's seven stages.

Open Research briefs
1Paper and notes 2Draft 3Your proof After the author

1Step 1: Paper and notes

Sign in with the desk password, then press New brief.

1New brief: starts one from the paper and your notes.

2Where it is: the seven stages, with the current one named.

3Waits for: the next thing to do, and since when.

4The title opens the brief's own page.

Once you choose the paper, the desk reads it and fills in the byline below.

1The paper, as a PDF: the desk reads it and fills in the byline.

2Your notes, as a file: they steer the draft.

3Paste instead: opens a box to paste or type the notes.

4Create the brief: opens once both files are in and the paper is read.

Finish the byline, then press Create the brief. It makes the brief's folder on the team drive and opens its page.

1Author's last name is the first author's. Change it to the Texas A&M author when that is someone else.

2Always add a Topic word. Authors repeat, and the desk can't rename the folder later.

3Put your own name in Brief author. This browser remembers it.

2Step 2: Draft

Press Write the draft. Notes typed in the box go with it, saved or not.

1Opens the brief's folder on the team drive.

2Your notes: they steer the draft.

3Write the draft: reads the paper, plans the pages, writes the brief. If you leave the page, Continue picks up where it stopped.

4In the folder: each file opens in Drive.

!If a step fails with a Drive or script message, see Bring back the Google scripts.

!If it fails again after Try again for any other reason, write to the desk's owner. READ ME FIRST - ERC handoff has the address.

When it is done, the stage is Your proof and the page shows the brief, its word count and its table or chart.

!Redraft writes the draft again from the paper, over brief-draft.md. It asks first. Use it before you edit.

Before you edit

  1. The pill beside the word count measures against 1,300 to 2,000 words. If it says short, add interpretation to a thin section, never a restatement.
  2. From here brief-draft.md in the folder is the brief, not the page. Every edit goes in the file.

3Step 3: Your proof

Proof the draft in the folder's files. The first time, make the Grammarly voice from grammarly-voice-setup.md in erc-research-briefs/.

Proof it

  1. Paste all of brief-for-grammarly.md into Grammarly. Choose ERC Research Brief Voice and run the Humanizer. The setup file lists what to check in each change.
  2. Settle each flag in check-notes.md. Keep a number as the paper prints it on the page named. Any you can't settle become questions for the author.
  3. On the brief's page, check the Brief table or chart against the Paper one beside it. Every number must match at the paper's precision. Fixes go in proposed-visual.md, in the folder's figures/ or the folder. REPORTING.md has the rest.

Put your proofed text back in the draft with Claude Code, opened on the ERC Research Briefs folder. In each prompt, swap in your folder's name.

Back into the draft

  1. Paste your text into Claude Code after this prompt: Put my text below into <your brief's folder>/brief-draft.md word for word. Keep its byline, headings, bold, [Table 1 here] or [Figure 1 here] line and references. List every change to a number, a hedge or a causal verb.
  2. Check each listed change against the paper.

The desk stops at Your proof. From here the brief's folder is the record.

With author

  1. Ask Claude Code: Run build-author-docx.js from erc-research-briefs on <your brief's folder>. If it says docx is missing, ask it to run npm install docx@9.7.1.
  2. It writes <your brief's folder>-author-review.docx into the folder. A gap shows in red as "Fix before sending". Never send a copy with a red line.
  3. Email the Word copy to the author of record with a reply date. Add any number questions from your proof.

After the author

Approved means the author's yes in writing. From then on the words are frozen.

Approved

  1. Save their reply in the folder under a new name, like <your brief's folder>-author-edits.docx, so a rebuild can't overwrite it. Never edit their file.
  2. Ask Claude Code: Number every tracked change and comment in <your brief's folder>/<your brief's folder>-author-edits.docx. Mark each Accept or Push back, with its rule from erc-research-briefs/VOICE.md or REPORTING.md. Change no file yet.
  3. Decide each one yourself. Accept facts, corrected numbers, added hedges and plainer wording. Push back on claims firmer than the paper, jargon, "we" and a dropped null result.
  4. Tell Claude which numbered changes to apply, then reread brief-draft.md. Reply to the author with a one-line reason for each change you didn't make.
  5. Send a second round only if a finding, a number or the table moved. Build the Word copy again for it.
  6. Save their yes in the folder as approved.pdf. In Outlook, choose File, Print, then PDF, Save as PDF.

Start only after the approval. Page breaks depend on the final words, and words move between pages, never cut.

HTML

  1. Ask Claude Code: Build <your brief's folder>/brief.html from a copy of erc-research-briefs/_template/brief-template.html, like erc-research-briefs/bowen-2026/brief.html. Follow STANDARDIZATION.md and REPORTING.md in erc-research-briefs. The visual is proposed-visual.md. Never cut or reword brief-draft.md to fit: give me options.
  2. Open brief.html in Chrome, press Cmd+P and choose Save as PDF: Letter, Margins None, Background graphics on, Headers and footers off. Print while online so the fonts load.
  3. Send Claude one problem per message, then print again.

If it looks wrong

  1. A page cut off, or white space under it: ask Claude for the options.
  2. Em dashes: press Cmd+F and type Option+Shift+hyphen. Search for "[" too, for unfilled placeholders.
  3. A broken logo: its path should start ../erc-research-briefs/assets/.

Put the PDF online, then send it through the desk like any other item.

Live

  1. Save the PDF in the folder, named like Economics-of-Encouragement-ERC-RB-26.pdf. In Drive, share it as Anyone with the link, Viewer, and copy the link.
  2. Add a row to ERC-Research-Briefs-Library-Metadata.xlsx in erc-research-briefs/, copying the rows above. Ask at the ERC who at the library takes it.
  3. On Submit content, paste the link and pick New research, then ERC Research. The brief's title is the Title, the Executive Summary's first paragraph the Description. Press Add to the queue.
  4. On Content Sort, fill any blank Date, Authors, Source or Topic. Leave ERC Newsletter only unticked and press Keep and next.
  5. On Newsletter's Next issue, press Add on it under Ready to add.
  6. Email the author of record the public link, with thanks.

What you need

  • The desk password. It is in READ ME FIRST - ERC handoff, in the shared drive.
