Keep or delete

Keep what helps someone make or study education policy. Delete the rest, and keep each thing once.

1The item the card shows. Pick a row, or move with the up and down arrows.

2Type: what the item is. Pick one, then its subtype.

3Keep and next: keeps it and opens the next item. K does the same. The line at the top then says where it waits next.

4Delete: takes it out of the queue. D does the same. Undo is on the line at the top, and on the grayed row at the bottom of the list.

About two in five papers, events and opportunities are kept, and one headline in five. An issue wants 3 to 5 outside research items or reports and 3 to 5 headlines.

Keep when

  1. The research tests a policy, such as teacher pay, attendance or pre-K.
  2. A headline reports a decision, a number or a finding, usually from a state or the federal government.
  3. A local story shows a wider trend: “Oklahoma School Closures Part of Statewide Picture of Enrollment Decline”.
  4. A policy webinar comes from a think tank or research group.
  5. It is an ERC event or an A&M research talk.
  6. It offers education researchers money, a fellowship or a call for proposals, with the deadline ahead.
  7. A person added it: their initials or name follow added by (bulk upload means none were given). Keep it unless it repeats.

Which type and subtype to pick

Delete it when any of these is true.

Delete when

  1. It is not about education.
  2. It is one bill or one rule notice.
  3. It is campus news, a scandal or a celebrity. College news that is state policy stays.
  4. It is one district's news with no wider point: “Austin ISD faces deeper budget cuts as enrollment declines”.
  5. It is opinion, an interview, a podcast, a guide or a roundup.
  6. A webinar teaches classroom practice or sells something.
  7. It is a job, an award or a conference listing. Grants with no deadline or for another field go too.
  8. The research has no policy in it: a method, a theory or a program description.
  9. It is a repeat. Keep the best copy.

Sources

Some sources have a clear rule, from how often they were kept.

A clear rule

  1. ECS State Policy Tracker, 0 of 54 kept: delete. Each is one state bill.
  2. Austin American-Statesman, 0 of 24: delete. Austin ISD and UT campus news.
  3. Urban Institute, 6 of 41: delete unless it is about schools.
  4. NBER, 3 of 18: delete unless it is about schooling.
  5. K-12 Dive, 6 of 17: keep findings and state counts. Delete Week In Review.
  6. EdWorkingPapers, 32 of 58: keep most. Delete methods papers.

Nearly every time

  1. Kept: CALDER, Texas Tribune, the Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board, the Tennessee Education Research Alliance, and ERC and A&M events.
  2. Deleted: Texas Public Radio, KUT, PBS NewsHour, the Department of Education newsroom, the Federal Register, WestEd and AIR.

!Possible duplicate names the earlier item. Not every repeat shows it.

Not sure? Press Skip for now. Clear Past and Already live with Dismiss all; one Undo brings a group back.

1Skipped: an item you parked. It waits under the new ones.

2Past: events and opportunities whose date or deadline has gone.

3Already live: links the Policy Exchange already has.

Finalize

Kept events, ERC events, opportunities, reports and research with no abstract get a description in the ERC's voice. Check each one here before it goes out.

1Needs a rewrite: kept items still waiting for their description.

2Rewrite the descriptions: writes the ones still waiting. The rewrite starts when you keep, so most arrive on their own.

3Original: the text it came with.

4Edit: write or fix the description by hand.

A rewrite that is back waits under To check, its changed words marked.

Check each rewrite

  1. Press Keep, or Use original for the item's own description.
  2. Fix a word with Edit. Saving the edit counts as checked.
  3. Keep the rest (N) keeps every rewrite still to check.
