# How it all connects
https://erc-content-desk.vercel.app/how-to/pipeline/

## How it all connects

Who sends an item, what the desk does with it, and where it ends up.

Three layers. On top, who sends and who reads: your team, anyone using the public form, the public site and the listserv inboxes. In the middle, the desk: Submit content, the Content queue, Content Sort, Finalize, then the Policy Exchange and the newsletter. Underneath, the parts: Drive and the scraper, the database with its Sheet copy, Claude, Outlook and GitHub. Filters on the way: you review a split doc, you pick the scraper files, a bot check, repeats stop, you keep, skip or delete, newsletter-only and already-live items stay off the Exchange, and you pick 3 to 5 of each for the newsletter. Beside it, sign-ups go to a Google Sheet and you add them to the listserv; research briefs are drafted by Claude and filed in Drive.

# How to sort
https://erc-content-desk.vercel.app/how-to/sort/

## Keep or delete

Keep what helps someone make or study education policy. Delete the rest, and keep each thing once.
 
1The item the card shows. Pick a row, or move with the up and down arrows.
 
2Type: what the item is. Pick one, then its subtype.
 
3Keep and next: keeps it and opens the next item. K does the same. The line at the top then says where it waits next.
 
4Delete: takes it out of the queue. D does the same. Undo is on the line at the top, and on the grayed row at the bottom of the list.

About two in five papers, events and opportunities are kept, and one headline in five. An issue wants 3 to 5 outside research items or reports and 3 to 5 headlines.

## Keep when

- The research tests a policy, such as teacher pay, attendance or pre-K.
 
- A headline reports a decision, a number or a finding, usually from a state or the federal government.
 
- A local story shows a wider trend: “Oklahoma School Closures Part of Statewide Picture of Enrollment Decline”.
 
- A policy webinar comes from a think tank or research group.
 
- It is an ERC event or an A&M research talk.
 
- It offers education researchers money, a fellowship or a call for proposals, with the deadline ahead.
 
- A person added it: their initials or name follow added by (bulk upload means none were given). Keep it unless it repeats.

Which type and subtype to pick (https://erc-content-desk.vercel.app/how-to/submit-content/#types)

Delete it when any of these is true.

## Delete when

- It is not about education.
 
- It is one bill or one rule notice.
 
- It is campus news, a scandal or a celebrity. College news that is state policy stays.
 
- It is one district's news with no wider point: “Austin ISD faces deeper budget cuts as enrollment declines”.
 
- It is opinion, an interview, a podcast, a guide or a roundup.
 
- A webinar teaches classroom practice or sells something.
 
- It is a job, an award or a conference listing. Grants with no deadline or for another field go too.
 
- The research has no policy in it: a method, a theory or a program description.
 
- It is a repeat. Keep the best copy.

## Sources

Some sources have a clear rule, from how often they were kept.

## A clear rule

- ECS State Policy Tracker, 0 of 54 kept: delete. Each is one state bill.
 
- Austin American-Statesman, 0 of 24: delete. Austin ISD and UT campus news.
 
- Urban Institute, 6 of 41: delete unless it is about schools.
 
- NBER, 3 of 18: delete unless it is about schooling.
 
- K-12 Dive, 6 of 17: keep findings and state counts. Delete Week In Review.
 
- EdWorkingPapers, 32 of 58: keep most. Delete methods papers.

## Nearly every time

- Kept: CALDER, Texas Tribune, the Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board, the Tennessee Education Research Alliance, and ERC and A&M events.
 
- Deleted: Texas Public Radio, KUT, PBS NewsHour, the Department of Education newsroom, the Federal Register, WestEd and AIR.

1Skipped: an item you parked. It waits under the new ones.

## Finalize

Kept events, ERC events, opportunities, reports and research with no abstract get a description in the ERC's voice. Check each one here before it goes out.
 
1Needs a rewrite: kept items still waiting for their description.
 
2Rewrite the descriptions: writes the ones still waiting. The rewrite starts when you keep, so most arrive on their own.
 
3Original: the text it came with.
 
4Edit: write or fix the description by hand.

A rewrite that is back waits under To check, its changed words marked.

## Check each rewrite

- Press Keep, or Use original for the item's own description.
 
- Fix a word with Edit. Saving the edit counts as checked.

# How to build the newsletter
https://erc-content-desk.vercel.app/how-to/newsletter/

## 1Step 1: Outline

On Next issue, Open the builder. Pick the issue's date: everything staged for it comes in, and anything new each time it opens.
 
1Issue: the date; picking it brings in what the desk holds for it. To practice, pick Sample issue (fictional) under Samples.
 
The draft is saved only in the browser you build it in. Finish the issue there.

## Where items come from

- Submit content: anyone on the team sends a link; it waits in the queue.
 
- Sort: each link gets a type and is kept, skipped or deleted.
 
- Finalize and Publish: the description is checked and the item goes on the Policy Exchange.
 
- Next issue: kept items wait under Ready to add. Add puts one in the issue; Quick add types in something new.
 
- Aim for 3 to 5 research items or reports from outside and 3 to 5 headlines, all policy relevant.
 
- The builder: picking the issue brings in what is in it.

Put the issue in the order it should print. Drag a section or an item by its grip; it lands where you drop it.
 
1A section's grip: drag the whole section up or down the email.
 
2An item's grip: drag it within its section.
 
3Move to…: file it under another section or group.
 
4Remove: takes it out. Undo on its row puts it back.

## 2Step 2: Preview & Tweak

The email as it will land in Outlook. Click anything on it to change it; what is not on it yet comes from the buttons above.
 
1Add an item: something the desk did not have.
 
2Add a callout: a box that asks readers for something.
 
3Contents strip: show or hide the row of section names.
 
4The contents strip on the email, one link per section.
 
Reset layout appears beside them once you change the section order, the contents strip or a look. It puts them all back.

Click an item and its card opens beside it. Every change shows on the email as you make it; Done closes the card.
 
1The item you clicked, lit on the email.
 
2How it is laid out: pick a look. Styles shows each one.
 
3Its words: title, link, description, date, time, place, media.
 
4Done closes the card. Undo changes puts it all back.

Reorder here too: point at an item or a section heading and drag it by the grip that appears at its left.
 
1The grip: it shows when you point at an item.
 
Fix things here, never in Outlook, so the archive matches what went out.

## 3Step 3: Save & Export

Copy HTML and send it (the next part). Once it has gone out, Save to the archive, so Past issues keeps it.
 
1Copy HTML: the whole email, ready for Outlook.
 
2Save to the archive: keeps it under Past issues. Off on a sample.
 
3Download .html: the same file, saved to your computer.
 
Start the next issue clears the draft for the next one.

## Send from erc@tamu.edu

The ERC mailbox can't install add-ins, and HTML pasted into a message as text never formats. So the newsletter goes into a message in your personal TAMU account through an add-in, and moves over to erc@tamu.edu from there.

## First time only: install the add-in

- In Outlook on the web, in your personal TAMU account, open a new message.
 
- Apps in the toolbar, or More apps, then Get Add-ins.
 
- Search Designmodo and add Insert HTML by Designmodo.

 The add-in on AppSource (https://appsource.microsoft.com/en-us/product/office/wa200002918)
 Microsoft: how to use add-ins in Outlook (https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/getstarted/use-add-ins-in-outlook)

Every issue: a new message in your personal account, and the add-in puts the newsletter in it.
 
Never paste the HTML into the message as text: it won't format.
 
Never open the .html in a browser and copy from there: wrong fonts.

## Put the newsletter in a message

- Copy HTML on Save & Export.
 
- In Outlook on the web, open a new message. Apps in the toolbar, or More apps, then Insert HTML by Designmodo.
 
- Paste into the add-in's Paste HTML box, then Insert HTML. The newsletter appears in the message.

The newsletter is now in a message in your account. Get it into one from erc@tamu.edu, either way, then send.

## Forward

- Send the message to erc@tamu.edu.
 
- Open it there and forward it to the list.
 
- Delete the forwarded-message lines at the top, then Send.

## Or copy

- In the message, select all and copy.
 
- Paste it into a new message from erc@tamu.edu, then Send.

# How to make a research brief
https://erc-content-desk.vercel.app/how-to/research-briefs/

## 1Step 1: Paper and notes

Sign in with the desk password, then press New brief.
 
1New brief: starts one from the paper and your notes.
 
2Where it is: the seven stages, with the current one named.
 
3Waits for: the next thing to do, and since when.
 
4The title opens the brief's own page.

Once you choose the paper, the desk reads it and fills in the byline below.
 
1The paper, as a PDF: the desk reads it and fills in the byline.
 
2Your notes, as a file: they steer the draft.
 
3Paste instead: opens a box to paste or type the notes.
 
4Create the brief: opens once both files are in and the paper is read.

Finish the byline, then press Create the brief. It makes the brief's folder on the team drive and opens its page.
 
1Author's last name is the first author's. Change it to the Texas A&M author when that is someone else.
 
2Always add a Topic word. Authors repeat, and the desk can't rename the folder later.
 
3Put your own name in Brief author. This browser remembers it.

 1Author's last name: starts the folder's name
 2Topic word: ends the folder's name
 3Brief author: named on the draft's cover

## 2Step 2: Draft

Press Write the draft. Notes typed in the box go with it, saved or not.
 
1Opens the brief's folder on the team drive.
 
2Your notes: they steer the draft.
 
3Write the draft: reads the paper, plans the pages, writes the brief. If you leave the page, Continue picks up where it stopped.
 
4In the folder: each file opens in Drive.
 
!If a step fails with a Drive or script message, see Bring back the Google scripts (https://erc-content-desk.vercel.app/how-to/google-scripts/).
 
!If it fails again after Try again for any other reason, write to the desk's owner. READ ME FIRST - ERC handoff has the address.

When it is done, the stage is Your proof and the page shows the brief, its word count and its table or chart.
 
!Redraft writes the draft again from the paper, over brief-draft.md. It asks first. Use it before you edit.

## Before you edit

- The pill beside the word count measures against 1,300 to 2,000 words. If it says short, add interpretation to a thin section, never a restatement.
 
- From here brief-draft.md in the folder is the brief, not the page. Every edit goes in the file.

## 3Step 3: Your proof

Proof the draft in the folder's files. The first time, make the Grammarly voice from grammarly-voice-setup.md in erc-research-briefs/.

## Proof it

- Paste all of brief-for-grammarly.md into Grammarly. Choose ERC Research Brief Voice and run the Humanizer. The setup file lists what to check in each change.
 
- Settle each flag in check-notes.md. Keep a number as the paper prints it on the page named. Any you can't settle become questions for the author.
 
- On the brief's page, check the Brief table or chart against the Paper one beside it. Every number must match at the paper's precision. Fixes go in proposed-visual.md, in the folder's figures/ or the folder. REPORTING.md has the rest.

Put your proofed text back in the draft with Claude Code, opened on the ERC Research Briefs folder. In each prompt, swap in your folder's name.

## Back into the draft

- Paste your text into Claude Code after this prompt: Put my text below into <your brief's folder>/brief-draft.md word for word. Keep its byline, headings, bold, [Table 1 here] or [Figure 1 here] line and references. List every change to a number, a hedge or a causal verb.
 
- Check each listed change against the paper.

## After the author

Approved means the author's yes in writing. From then on the words are frozen.

## Approved

- Save their reply in the folder under a new name, like <your brief's folder>-author-edits.docx, so a rebuild can't overwrite it. Never edit their file.
 
- Ask Claude Code: Number every tracked change and comment in <your brief's folder>/<your brief's folder>-author-edits.docx. Mark each Accept or Push back, with its rule from erc-research-briefs/VOICE.md or REPORTING.md. Change no file yet.
 
- Decide each one yourself. Accept facts, corrected numbers, added hedges and plainer wording. Push back on claims firmer than the paper, jargon, "we" and a dropped null result.
 
- Tell Claude which numbered changes to apply, then reread brief-draft.md. Reply to the author with a one-line reason for each change you didn't make.
 
- Send a second round only if a finding, a number or the table moved. Build the Word copy again for it.
 
- Save their yes in the folder as approved.pdf. In Outlook, choose File, Print, then PDF, Save as PDF.

Start only after the approval. Page breaks depend on the final words, and words move between pages, never cut.
