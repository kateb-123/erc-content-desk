## Keep or delete

Keep what helps someone make or study education policy. Delete the rest, and keep each thing once.
 
1The item the card shows. Pick a row, or move with the up and down arrows.
 
2Type: what the item is. Pick one, then its subtype.
 
3Keep and next: keeps it and opens the next item. K does the same. The line at the top then says where it waits next.
 
4Delete: takes it out of the queue. D does the same. Undo is on the line at the top, and on the grayed row at the bottom of the list.

Guess on each real card from the queue, then see its group. About two in five papers, events and opportunities are kept, and one headline in five.

## Keep when

- Tests or changes a policy: research, decisions, wider trends. The research tests a policy, such as teacher pay, attendance or pre-K. A headline reports a decision, a number or a finding, usually from a state or the federal government. A local story shows a wider trend: “Oklahoma School Closures Part of Statewide Picture of Enrollment Decline”.
 
- Policy events and money: policy and research conferences, webinars, calls and grants. It is a conference on education policy or research, or its call for proposals. A policy webinar comes from a think tank or research group. It offers education researchers money, a fellowship or a call for proposals, with the deadline ahead.
 
- Ours or added by hand: ERC and A&M events, a person's pick. It is an ERC event or an A&M research talk. A person added it: their initials or name follow added by (bulk upload means none were given). Keep it unless it repeats.

## Delete when any of these is true

- Not education policy: off topic, or one campus, district or bill. It is not about education. It is one bill or one rule notice. It is campus news, a scandal or a celebrity. College news that is state policy stays. It is one district's news with no wider point: “Austin ISD faces deeper budget cuts as enrollment declines”. The research has no policy in it: a method, a theory or a program description.
 
- Not news we share: opinion, roundups, jobs, awards, teaching or trade conferences. It is opinion, an interview, a podcast, a guide or a roundup. A webinar teaches classroom practice or sells something. It is a job, an award, or a teaching or trade conference. Grants with no deadline or for another field go too.
 
- Already in the queue: a second copy of an item. It is a repeat. Keep the best copy.

## Read me

The newsletter's HTML: the two pages, and when to change the template itself.

## The two pages

- Blank template (https://erc-content-desk.vercel.app/newsletter/template/): every section and callout, with placeholder words in each slot, so you can see every place an item can go.
 
- Sample issue (https://erc-content-desk.vercel.app/newsletter/sample/): a fictional issue with every kind of item, as the builder would send it. The builder's Issue list offers the same sample to practice on.

Both are drawn from the builder's own template the moment they open, so they always match what the builder makes. Copy HTML on either page puts that email's code on the clipboard.

## Change the template itself

Only for a big style change that every issue after should carry: the masthead, the colors and type, a new section, a new layout. The email's look lives in one file of the desk's code, builder/js/template.js, and a change there reaches the builder, the blank template and the sample at once. Open the desk's folder in Claude Code and ask for the change; it writes the code and runs the desk's tests, and the change goes live when it is pushed. Example asks:

- "Add a Podcasts section after Headlines, with its own label."
 
- "Change the masthead to the new ERC banner."
 
- "Use a darker maroon for every section rule and button."

A change to one issue belongs in the builder: see Build the newsletter (https://erc-content-desk.vercel.app/how-to/newsletter/). Who can push a change: Who to ask (https://erc-content-desk.vercel.app/how-to/change-the-desk/#ask).

## Before you edit

- The pill beside the word count measures against 1,300 to 2,000 words. If it says short, add interpretation to a thin section, never a restatement.
 
- From here brief-draft.md in the folder is the brief, not the page. Every edit goes in the file.

## 3Step 3: Your proof

Proof the draft in the folder's files. The first time, make the Grammarly voice from grammarly-voice-setup.md in erc-research-briefs/.

## Proof it

- Paste all of brief-for-grammarly.md into Grammarly. Choose ERC Research Brief Voice and run the Humanizer. The setup file lists what to check in each change.
 
- Settle each flag in check-notes.md. Keep a number as the paper prints it on the page named. Any you can't settle become questions for the author.
 
- On the brief's page, check the Brief table or chart against the Paper one beside it. Every number must match at the paper's precision. Fixes go in proposed-visual.md, in the folder's figures/ or the folder. REPORTING.md has the rest.

Put your proofed text back in the draft with Claude Code, opened on the ERC Research Briefs folder. In each prompt, swap in your folder's name.

## Back into the draft

- Paste your text into Claude Code after this prompt: Put my text below into <your brief's folder>/brief-draft.md word for word. Keep its byline, headings, bold, [Table 1 here] or [Figure 1 here] line and references. List every change to a number, a hedge or a causal verb.
 
- Check each listed change against the paper.

The desk stops at Your proof. From here the brief's folder is the record.

## With author

- Ask Claude Code: Run build-author-docx.js from erc-research-briefs on <your brief's folder>. If it says docx is missing, ask it to run npm install docx@9.7.1.
 
- It writes <your brief's folder>-author-review.docx into the folder. A gap shows in red as "Fix before sending". Never send a copy with a red line.
 
- Email the Word copy to the author of record with a reply date. Add any number questions from your proof.

## After the author

Approved means the author's yes in writing. From then on the words are frozen.

## Approved

- Save their reply in the folder under a new name, like <your brief's folder>-author-edits.docx, so a rebuild can't overwrite it. Never edit their file.
 
- Ask Claude Code: Number every tracked change and comment in <your brief's folder>/<your brief's folder>-author-edits.docx. Mark each Accept or Push back, with its rule from erc-research-briefs/VOICE.md or REPORTING.md. Change no file yet.
 
- Decide each one yourself. Accept facts, corrected numbers, added hedges and plainer wording. Push back on claims firmer than the paper, jargon, "we" and a dropped null result.
 
- Tell Claude which numbered changes to apply, then reread brief-draft.md. Reply to the author with a one-line reason for each change you didn't make.
 
- Send a second round only if a finding, a number or the table moved. Build the Word copy again for it.
 
- Save their yes in the folder as approved.pdf. In Outlook, choose File, Print, then PDF, Save as PDF.

Start only after the approval. Page breaks depend on the final words, and words move between pages, never cut.

https://erc-content-desk.vercel.app/how-to/design/

## Claude Design

The Aggie Web Template is Texas A&M's web design system, for web pages. For flyers and posts, use Canva.

## Start a web page

- The first time, open the Aggie Web Template link in the email.
 
- In Claude Design, say "use the Aggie Web Template design system".

## Canva

Aggie Maroon carries most of a design.

## First time only: the Brand Kit

- In Canva, open Brand, then Brand Kit.
 
- Add each color in the table under these slides by its HEX.

Fonts: Oswald for big titles in capitals, Work Sans for headings, Open Sans for body.
 
!Never redraw, recolor or stretch a logo.
 
!Put a logo only on white or maroon.

## Logos

- Download the ERC logo pack for screens and unzip it.
 
- Upload the logos in it to Canva.

ERC logo pack for screens (https://mysehd.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/ERC-logo-pack-RGB-for-web.zip)

## Print

In print, Aggie Maroon is CMYK 15 100 39 69, Pantone 7421.

## Before it goes to the printer

- Use the ERC logo pack for print.
 
- Set body text at 11 point or larger.
 
- From Canva, download as PDF Print, with bleed if the printer asks.

ERC logo pack for print (https://mysehd.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/ERC-logo-pack-CMYK-for-print.zip)
