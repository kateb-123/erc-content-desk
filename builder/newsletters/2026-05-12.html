Research Brief

Into DEEP:
Finding Schools Where Black Students Thrive in Texas

John A. Williams III (Texas A&M University)  |  Sonyia C. Richardson (Univ. of North Carolina at Chapel Hill)  |  Chance W. Lewis (Univ. of North Carolina at Charlotte)

Black students in Texas face the highest suspension rates of any racial group and consistently underperform peers on state assessments. This brief set out to identify the schools that are bucking both trends at once. Using state and federal data from 2020–2021, researchers found only 87 of 3,988 urban schools in Texas (2%) met both benchmarks for academic performance and proportional suspension rates for Black students. These “DEEP schools” clustered near the Texas-Mexico border and around Houston and Dallas, had more diverse teaching staff than average, but most lacked full-time counselors, social workers, or psychologists. The findings show what’s possible for Black students and point to where more investment is needed.
