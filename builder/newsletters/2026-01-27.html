This month’s ERC Spotlight is a fun one: we’re thrilled to introduce our new Graduate Student Ambassadors program and share information about the next ERC Happy Hour.

Now Recruiting - ERC Graduate Student Ambassadors! 

The Education Research Center is launching an interdisciplinary Graduate Student Ambassadors group to help expand ERC’s reach, programming, and partnerships.

Ambassadors will support website and tech projects, marketing and recruitment, event planning, mentoring undergraduate students, and project management for ERC initiatives (about 5 hours per week).


This is not a research assistantship, but a great way to get involved with education policy work, build relationships, and position yourself for future ERC research opportunities.


Eligibility: Any current TAMU master’s or doctoral student with an interest in education policy, strong initiative, and the ability to work independently and remotely.


To apply, submit your CV and a short paragraph (max 250 words) describing how your background fits this role and/or why you’re interested in serving as an ERC Graduate Student Ambassador.

Review of Applications begins Feb. 20th!

Carter-Larke Black History and Education Lecture

February 23, 2026  | 11:00am - 2:00pm

Memorial Student Center, Room 2300

Learn more and RSVP

The Carter-Larke Black History and Education Lecture honors the legacies of Dr. Norvella Carter and Dr. Patricia Larke, two trailblazing faculty whose scholarship and teaching transformed generations of educators. Since 2017, it has grown into a signature Black History Month event in the College of Education and Human Development. 


This year’s lecture, Nine Beats Strong: Rhythm, Resistance, and Renaissance, offers an engaging program exploring Black history, culture, and education through the lens of rhythm and resistance. Hear from keynote speaker Dr. Tony B. Watlington Sr., superintendent of the School District of Philadelphia, and join a campus-wide conversation on educational justice and renewal.

IES Summer Research Training Institute on Cluster-Randomized Trials

The IES Summer Research Training Institute on Cluster-Randomized Trials at Northwestern University offers intensive, hands-on training in planning, implementing, and analyzing CRTs in education. The institute covers causal inference, multilevel and nested designs, measurement and implementation fidelity, and applied data analysis using statistical software. Participants will leave better equipped to design and lead rigorous evaluations of education interventions.


Dates: July 6-16th, 2026

Location: Northwestern University, Chicago, IL
