FROM THE ERC

 
 

NEW GRADUATE COURSE AUDIT FOR EDUCATION POLICY

The Education Research Center is excited to share a Graduate Course Audit for Education Policy, a new resource designed to help students, advisors, and faculty navigate policy-relevant coursework across Texas A&M. This spreadsheet identifies graduate courses that may be especially useful for students interested in education policy, education research, or systems-level change and is intended to support thoughtful course planning, advising, and professional skill development.


The audit includes a categorized list of graduate courses, definitions of policy-related competency areas, and a submission form for suggesting additional courses or revisions. Courses are organized by key themes such as research skills, policy analysis, communication & influence, and systems knowledge to help users better understand how different offerings build complementary skill sets.


While this resource is not intended to be exhaustive and reflects courses identified during the current academic year, we hope it serves as a practical starting point for students and advisors. Students are encouraged to consult with their advisors to confirm alignment with degree requirements and individual academic goals.


If you know of a course that should be included or see an opportunity for refinement, we welcome your input through the course submission form linked in the audit.


We encourage you to explore the audit and share it with students and colleagues who may find it useful!

TEXAS A&M INTERNAL OPPORTUNITIES

 

Research Excellence Fund

The Texas A&M University System has launched a new Research Excellence Fund to accelerate research growth, collaboration, and competitiveness across System institutions. With an initial $35 million annual investment, the REF will support collaborative discovery grants, graduate fellowships, faculty hiring and retention, shared research equipment, proposal cost share, and early-stage research development—prioritizing projects aligned with state and national research priorities and positioned for large-scale external funding.

Teaching Excellence Grant Program

The College of Education & Human Development is now accepting proposals for the Teaching Excellence Grant Program. With awards starting at $15,000, TEGP supports innovative, high-impact teaching practices, student engagement in the scholarship of teaching and learning, and improvements to distance education. Projects should address current undergraduate and/or graduate student learning needs and may include evaluative components, though a formal research design is not required.


Internal submission deadline: Friday, January 30, 2026.

WILLIAM T. GRANT FOUNDATION

 

Research Grants on Improving the Use of Research Evidence

The Foundation seeks proposals for studies that examine strategies to boost the use, usefulness, and impact of research evidence in policy and practice — especially within youth-serving systems (young people ages 5–25). Successful projects will advance theory or build empirical knowledge about how decision-makers (policymakers, agency leaders, intermediaries, etc.) can effectively integrate research into decisions — and whether that use improves youth outcomes.


Funding ranges from $100,000–$1,000,000 (2–4 years) for major grants, and $25,000–$50,000 (1–2 years) for Officers’ research grants.

Research Grants on Improving the Use of Research Evidence

The Foundation invites proposals for research projects that build, test, or deepen understanding of programs, policies, or practices aimed at reducing inequality in academic, social, behavioral, or economic outcomes for youth (ages 5–25) in the U.S. Grants support descriptive, intervention, or evaluation studies focused on inequities tied to race, ethnicity, socioeconomic status, immigrant or language background, or sexual/gender minority status.


Funding ranges from $25,000–$50,000 (1–2 years, Officers’ grants) to $100,000–$600,000 (2–3 years, Major grants).
