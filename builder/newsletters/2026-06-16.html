Research Brief

Economics of Encouragement: Can a Single Email Shape What Students Study?

Olivia Edwards & John Meer

Can a single email shape what students study? Among 10,000+ students in a large introductory economics course at Texas A&M, high-performing students who received a short note from a professor encouraging further study were more likely to enroll in the next economics course. The effect was largest for first-generation and underrepresented students — a low-cost nudge with real equity potential.

Research Brief

Equity in Practice: How New Teachers Bring Fairness Into the Classroom

Andrew Kwok, Jenna Davis, Sara Halabi, Joseph Waddington, Debbee Huston, Rita Hemsley

Are new teachers prepared to teach fairly in increasingly diverse classrooms? Across nearly 1,900 survey responses from new teachers in one of California’s largest induction programs, only a small share described their work with a clear equity focus. Those who did mapped a path from including diverse voices to addressing systemic inequities — concrete practices that preparation programs can teach.

Research Brief

Managing without Mandate: A Nationwide Review of State Training Laws

Megan Svajda-Hardy & Andrew Kwok

How much do state laws actually support teachers in managing classroom behavior? A review of discipline statutes across all 50 states and DC found only 31 statutes in 21 states that address support for general classroom teachers. Most name who provides training and who’s covered, but few require it or specify how often — leaving classroom management largely unmandated.

NEW EDUCATION POLICY RESEARCH

Property Tax Salience and Public Good Investment: Evidence from School Bond Elections

EdWorkingPaper  |  June 2026

After Texas began requiring school bond ballots to state “THIS IS A PROPERTY TAX INCREASE,” bond passage rates fell about 9 percentage points. The warning made property taxes more salient and eroded support — an effect concentrated in lower-information November elections.

 

Immigration Enforcement Actions and Empty Desks: Persistent and Acute Attendance Effects

EdWorkingPaper  |  April 2026

Heightened federal immigration enforcement after the January 2025 inauguration drove a sustained 37% rise in absences among foreign-born students in one mid-size district, with effects growing by grade level.

 

AI in PK-12 Education: Implications for Teaching and Learning

MERC Research & Policy Review  |  May 2026

This review from the Metropolitan Educational Research Consortium synthesizes how AI is reshaping instruction and student learning in PK-12 classrooms, closing with key takeaways and recommendations for policy and practice.

 

Trends in Local Teacher Supply Since the COVID-19 Pandemic: Evidence from Teacher Job Applications

EdWorkingPaper  |  May 2026

Using job-application data from a large district (2015–2023), applications per posting fell 52–75% below pre-pandemic projections starting in 2021 — across both hard- and easy-to-staff subjects and higher- and lower-poverty schools.

 

Teacher Certification in Texas: District Strategies to Recruit and Retain a Credentialed Workforce

Learning Policy Institute report  |  May 2026

The share of uncertified teachers in Texas more than tripled between 2019–20 and 2024–25, yet some districts kept certification rates high. Drawing on six such districts, the report highlights the recruitment, hiring, and partnership practices — along with HB2’s new PREP Allotment funding — linked to retaining certified teachers.

 

The Effects of High-Impact Tutoring on Student Attendance: Evidence from a State Initiative

EdWorkingPaper (forthcoming in AERA Open)  |  June 2026

Students in Washington DC’s High-Impact Tutoring initiative were 7% less likely to be absent on days they were scheduled for tutoring, with larger effects when sessions combined small groups, frequency, and in-school delivery.

 

Identifying Effective Attendance Strategies in Michigan

Report — Detroit Partnership for Education Equity & Research (PEER)  |  May 2026

Pairing a school-level “attendance value-added” measure with surveys of school leaders, this report examines which attendance practices, staffing, and leadership approaches are linked to stronger student attendance in Michigan — isolating schools’ own contributions from the broader conditions that shape attendance.
